01 · Vision

Every orchard worked by an autonomous workforce

Orchard work will be automated. Tully builds the robots that make it reliable and affordable for growers, season after season, and sells the finished work, not the machines.

Where we start

  • ApplesThe largest labour pool of any tree fruit
  • Thinning, then harvestingThinning is simpler and reaches farmers sooner; harvesting uses the same machine
  • Europe's dense apple regionsSouth Tyrol and Bodensee first

Where we grow

  • More tasksPruning, and partner robots for spraying, mowing, scouting and bin logistics, all planned by one system
  • More tree fruitPears and other pome fruit, stone fruit, citrus, avocados
  • More regionsHub by hub, and the Southern Hemisphere for a second season a year

What we leave out

  • Berries, vegetables, greenhousesDifferent crops, different machines
  • Selling machinesFarmers book the work; Tully runs the fleet

06 · Business model

Farmers book the work, not the machine

The farmer books

  • One orderThe blocks, the timing and the quality required
  • Paid per hectareCompares directly with a seasonal crew
  • Nothing to buyNo machine, no upfront investment

Tully delivers

  • The work, doneTully deploys, operates, supervises and maintains the robots
  • Responsible for the resultQuality of the work and the work done, against agreed requirements
  • A fleet busy all seasonThinning, then harvesting: more revenue than selling a robot once

Price per hectare

Thinning≈1,320€per hectare, against ≈1,470 € by hand: 105 h at 14 € an hour
Harvesting5,576€per hectare, against 6,195 € for a team of six on a picking platform

Both 10 % below the work by hand. Hours: Laimburg, 2026. Wage: South Tyrol farm workers' agreement, 12.11 € an hour gross, plus employer costs

Run from regional hubs

  1. Orchards clusterClimate and terroir pack fruit farms into dense regions
