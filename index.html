End of arm

Hand D: cut first, twist and pull if the stem is hidden

  1. Head camera and ToF: fruit size sets the stand-off; the stem-visibility score picks the mode.
  2. Three fingers close, each on its own lead screw, and stop at 26 N; the screws self-lock.
  3. Fingertip cameras see the hidden back of the fruit: colour check and stem check.
  4. Stem seen: the cutting finger swings 170° off the deck; the guard shoe rides the shoulder and the V takes the stem.
  5. Throat beam breaks, pull 8 mm, valve fires: the blade turns 50° and shears the stem 5–11 mm above the cavity rim.
  6. Stem hidden, or the beam never breaks after a ±8° search: pull 10 mm, J6 rolls the hand 70°, the stem lets go at the spur.
  7. Cutter parks, blade springs back, J6 rolls back; the fruit goes to delivery.

    lane capacity, single file at 0.25 m/s
    1. Both landing belts run inboard and tip onto a braked ramp into one single-file roller lane.
    2. Under the glass hood the rollers spin each fruit while two cameras grade all of its skin: fresh or industry.
    3. At the gate the flaps let fresh fruit into the throat of the soft filler: a padded, brush-lined spiral that hangs from the deck into the bin.
    4. Fruit roll down 2.5 turns at ≤ 0.35 m/s, cross a slowly turning padded spreader and step 5 cm onto the pile. No free fall anywhere.
    5. The carrier's hoist lifts the empty bin 0.45 m up round the spiral and lowers it as the pile grows; the spiral shortens for the last 0.17 m.
    6. Industry fruit roll over the closed flaps to the end of the lane, down the chute and a braked sock into the crate (they go to juice).
